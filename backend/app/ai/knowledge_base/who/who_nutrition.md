Core Principles of a Healthy Diet
Eat at least 400 grams (five portions) of fruit and vegetables every day, excluding starchy roots like potatoes or cassava
. A healthy diet should include whole grains like unprocessed maize, oats, and brown rice, along with legumes such as lentils and beans
. Total fat intake should stay below 30% of your total energy intake to prevent unhealthy weight gain
. It is important to shift from saturated fats—found in butter, cheese, and fatty meats—to healthier unsaturated fats found in fish, avocados, and nuts
.
Industrially-produced trans fats found in many fried and processed snacks are not part of a healthy diet and should be avoided
. Aim for less than 10% of your energy from saturated fats and less than 1% from trans fats
. Balancing the calories you consume with the energy you use through physical activity is essential for maintaining a healthy weight
. Incorporating fresh, seasonal produce and a variety of food choices helps ensure you get enough dietary fibre and essential nutrients to protect against malnutrition and chronic diseases
.
Managing Sodium and Salt Intake
For adults, daily salt intake should be kept under 5 grams, which is approximately one teaspoon
. This is equivalent to less than 2 grams of sodium per day
. Following these limits helps lower blood pressure and significantly reduces the risk of heart disease and stroke
. For children aged 2 to 15, the maximum intake of 2 grams of sodium should be adjusted downward based on their lower energy requirements compared to adults
.
Most dietary salt comes from processed foods like bread, ready-made meals, and salty snacks, or it is added during cooking with items like bouillon cubes and soy sauce
. You can reduce salt consumption by choosing products with lower sodium content, checking food labels before purchasing, and not keeping salt on the table
. Using iodized salt is also recommended to ensure adequate iodine intake
. Increasing your intake of potassium through fresh fruits and vegetables can help lessen the negative effects of high sodium on blood pressure
. Aiming for a one-to-one molar ratio of sodium to potassium is considered highly beneficial for health
.
Sugar Intake and Health Risks
Limiting the intake of free sugars throughout life is a key part of maintaining health
. For both adults and children, free sugars should make up less than 10% of total energy intake
. This is roughly 50 grams or about 12 level teaspoons for an average adult
. Reducing this further to less than 5% provides additional health benefits
. Free sugars include all sugars added to foods and drinks by the manufacturer, cook, or consumer
. They are also naturally present in honey, syrups, fruit juices, and fruit juice concentrates
.
Consuming too much free sugar increases the risk of dental caries (tooth decay) and contributes to unhealthy weight gain, which can lead to overweight and obesity
. To lower your sugar intake, limit your consumption of sugar-sweetened beverages, candies, and sugary snacks
. Instead, choose fresh fruits and raw vegetables as healthy snack alternatives
. Be aware that many processed foods contain high amounts of hidden sugars, so checking nutritional labels is an effective way to monitor and reduce your daily intake
.
Nutrition for Infants and Young Children
Optimal nutrition in the first two years of life is vital for healthy growth, improved cognitive development, and reducing the risk of obesity and chronic diseases later in life
. Infants should be breastfed exclusively for the first six months of life to provide all the essential nutrients they need
. Continued breastfeeding should ideally occur until the child is two years old or beyond
.
Starting at six months of age, breast milk should be complemented with a variety of safe, nutrient-dense foods
. It is critical that salt and sugars are not added to these complementary foods, as healthy dietary habits and taste preferences are established very early in life
. While the general principles for a healthy diet—such as eating plenty of fruits and vegetables and limiting unhealthy fats—apply to children as well as adults, these early breastfeeding practices are unique to infants
. Providing a variety of adequate and safe foods ensures young children develop a palate for natural flavours rather than processed, salty, or sugary items
.
