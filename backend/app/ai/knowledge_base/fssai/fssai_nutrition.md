Achieving a Balanced Diet with 'My Plate'
A balanced diet provides essential calories, proteins, vitamins, minerals, and fibre
. The 'My Plate for the Day' model recommends sourcing nutrients from at least eight different food groups
. Essentially, half of the daily plate should be filled with a variety of vegetables, green leafy vegetables, roots, tubers, and fruits
. The remaining portion should consist of cereals and millets, followed by pulses, flesh foods, or eggs, and milk or curd
.
To maintain health, aim to consume foods from at least 5 to 7 food groups daily
. Nutritional guidelines specify that cereals and millets should provide no more than 45% of your total daily calories
. Pulses, beans, and meat should contribute up to 15% of daily energy
. The rest of your calories should ideally come from nutrient-dense sources like nuts, vegetables, fruits, and dairy
. In a standard 2000 Kcal diet, this breakdown ensures about 50–55% of energy comes from carbohydrates, 10–15% from proteins, and 20–30% from dietary fats
. Choosing whole grains or minimally polished grains for at least 50% of your cereal intake provides necessary fibre
.
Managing Salt, Sugar, and Fats
Restricting the intake of salt, sugar, and fats is critical for preventing non-communicable diseases
. Daily salt intake should be restricted to less than 5g per day, which is roughly one teaspoon
. High salt consumption is directly linked to hypertension and heart disease
. For sugar, adults should restrict intake to less than 5% of their total daily energy, which is approximately 20 to 25g per day
. It is highly recommended to avoid added sugar entirely for children under two years of age
.
When using fats and oils, moderation is key. Total fat intake should not exceed 30% of total daily energy
. Visible cooking oils should be limited to 25 to 30g per day for an average adult
. Choose vegetable oils containing essential fatty acids and limit the use of saturated fats like butter or ghee to just 1 to 2 teaspoons per day
. Avoid partially hydrogenated vegetable oils (vanaspathi) and do not reuse oil that has been repeatedly heated, as it generates harmful compounds
. Minimize "High Fat, Sugar, and Salt" (HFSS) foods and ultra-processed items like chips, biscuits, and sugary sodas
.
Nutrition During Pregnancy and Lactation
Pregnancy and lactation are nutritionally demanding periods that require extra food to support both the mother and the developing baby
. A pregnant woman needs an additional 350 Kcal per day to ensure normal weight gain
. It is recommended that a woman be at least 21 years old at the time of her first pregnancy
. Diet should include a wide variety of food groups, emphasizing pulses, nuts, milk, eggs, and flesh foods to ensure adequate protein and essential fatty acids
.
Specific micronutrients like iron and folic acid are vital
. Daily supplementation of 60mg elemental iron and 0.5mg folic acid is recommended starting from the 12th week of pregnancy and continuing through the first six months of lactation
. Green leafy vegetables, legumes, and nuts are excellent natural sources of folic acid
. To support hydration and digestion, pregnant and lactating women should consume more than two litres of fluid daily
. It is crucial to avoid alcohol and tobacco in any form during these stages
. High-sugar and high-salt processed foods should also be minimized to maintain a healthy body mass index and blood pressure
.
Infant and Child Nutrition
Infants should be exclusively breastfed for the first six months
. After six months, complementary foods should be introduced while continuing breastfeeding
. These complementary meals must be nutrient-dense and fed at frequent intervals, typically four times a day, because infants cannot consume large portions at once
. Do not add any salt or sugar to an infant's food
. Recommended early foods include mashed fruits like banana or papaya and purees made from rice and dals
.
For children above two years and adolescents, a balanced diet is essential for optimal growth and immunity
. Their meals should include a combination of whole grains, pulses, and milk or curd
. Combining cereals with pulses in a ratio of 3:1 improves the quality of protein consumed
. Ensure children consume at least 50% of their grains as whole grains and include plenty of fresh, locally available vegetables
. Avoid packaged snacks and beverages with added sugar, such as fruit juices and carbonated drinks, which increase the risk of obesity and metabolic disorders
. Regular physical activity and outdoor play are also necessary for healthy development
.
Health and Wellness for the Elderly
As people age, their metabolism slows and physical activity often decreases, leading to lower energy requirements
. However, the need for vitamins and minerals remains high to maintain health and quality of life
. Elderly individuals should focus on nutrient-rich foods like pulses, legumes, low-fat milk, eggs, and lean meat
. Energy intake should be approximately 1740 Kcal for men and 1530 Kcal for women over 60 years of age
.
Food preparations for the elderly should be well-cooked, soft, and easy to chew
. Salt should be restricted, and spices should be used in moderate amounts
. Drinking adequate water—at least two litres per day—is essential to prevent dehydration and constipation
. Regular physical activity, such as walking or yoga, is highly recommended to maintain muscle mass, bone density, and brain function while managing body weight
. Avoiding ultra-processed foods and items high in fat, sugar, and salt can help delay the onset of age-related degenerative diseases
. Including plenty of fruits and vegetables ensures sufficient fibre intake and satiety
.
Food Safety, Hydration, and Labelling
Drinking adequate water is fundamental to health. A healthy person should drink approximately eight glasses (two litres) of water and other healthy beverages per day
. Prefer water, buttermilk, tender coconut water, or fresh lemon juice over synthetic soft drinks and carbonated beverages
. If the safety of the water supply is in doubt, always boil water before consumption
. When consuming fruit, it is better to eat the whole fresh fruit rather than drinking juice; if juice is consumed, limit it to the equivalent of 100-150g of fruit occasionally
.
Always read food labels to make informed choices
. Check the 'Nutrition Facts' for energy (Kcal), protein, carbohydrates, total sugar, and fat content
. Pay close attention to serving sizes, as one package may contain multiple servings
. For solid foods, look for items that contain less than 250 Kcal, less than 3g of added sugar, and less than 625mg of salt per 100g
. For liquid beverages, the threshold for a healthy choice is less than 70 Kcal and less than 2g of added sugar per 100ml
. Avoid products containing trans fats and select those low in saturated fats
.
